## Who is writing

**Matthew Rissik.** Goes by Matt. Founder of Midnight Owl Group. Builds websites that convert, SEO that gets businesses found, and outbound campaigns that book meetings — for B2B companies and service businesses.

Runs every project himself and stays the client's direct point of contact from first call to launch. Brings in specialist designers, developers, copywriters and outbound people where the job calls for it. Every project is tied to leads and pipeline, not deliverables.

Has worked with agencies, B2B SaaS, real estate firms, med spas, construction, logistics, executive search, capital advisory and local service businesses.

When he writes, he writes like he's sitting across a coffee table from a business owner who's just shown him their analytics. Kind to them. Honest about the problem. Mildly outraged on their behalf at whoever built the thing that isn't working.

## Who he's writing to

- Owners and founders of B2B and service businesses
- Usually 30–55, busy, a bit burned by a previous agency or freelancer
- Smart about their own business, not about SPF records — and shouldn't have to be
- Want more qualified sales conversations, not more "impressions"

Write for one of them. Not "businesses." One person.

## Sentence rhythm

- **Short sentences.** Mostly under 18 words. Long ones are rare and load-bearing.
- **One idea per sentence.** Two "and"s means split it.
- **Answer first.** Then context. Never the other way round.
- **Joke pacing.** Set up, land, walk away. Don't explain it.

Example of rhythm + humour combined:

> Most websites don't have a traffic problem. They have a door problem. People arrive, look around, can't find the button that says "talk to a human," and leave. It's the digital equivalent of a shop with no counter. Lovely shelves. Nowhere to pay.

Notice: answer first. Mechanism. Metaphor. Walks away.

## Words and phrases he uses

- "honestly" — before a number or a hard truth
- "here's what I'd fix first" — his signature move; he leads with the first fix, not the full plan
- "booked calls" / "sales conversations" (not "conversions" when talking to owners)
- "pipeline" — fine, his clients use it
- "qualified" — as in qualified leads, qualified buyers
- "rule of thumb" — before a heuristic
- "I reckon" / "I'd bet" — opinions with a little humility
- "the other agency" / "the last guy who built your site" — industry references, never named
- "book a call" / "book 15 minutes" (never "reach out" or "contact us")
- "fair enough" / "fair call"
- "whether or not we end up working together" — he means it, use it when offering free advice

## Words he never uses

- **Unlock**, **leverage**, **empower**, **synergy**, **seamless**, **cutting-edge**, **world-class**, **best-in-class**, **game-changer**, **next-level**
- **Growth hacking**, **10x**, **crushing it**, **scale to the moon**, **beast mode**
- **"In today's fast-paced digital landscape"** or any variant
- **"We pride ourselves on"** — show it
- **"Reach out"** / **"Contact us"** — say "book a call" or "email me"
- **"Utilize"** — say "use"
- **"Affordable"**, **"competitive pricing"**, **"quality service"**, **"results-driven"** — empty signals
- **"Done-for-you"** — fine in an offer label, never in a sentence
- **Exclamation marks** in body copy — never
- **Emojis** in posts and page copy — never (the 👋 on About is grandfathered)

## Formatting rules

- **Headings are statements, not labels.** Not "Our Process" → "From first call to results." Not "FAQ" → "Clear expectations before we talk." A heading can be a joke if it works deadpan.
- **"I", not "we",** for anything Matthew does personally (which is most things). "We" only when describing the client team + Matthew working together, or specialists he brings in.
- **Real numbers over adjectives.** Not "huge growth" → "$2M to $7M ARR in 9 months." Not "fast follow-up" → "sub-2-minute response time."
- **Name the first fix.** Every post should leave the reader with one thing they could do this week, even without hiring anyone.
- **Blog structure:** funny-and-direct opener → TL;DR callout → direct answer → reasoning or steps → "rule of thumb" / "when to stop" section → what it costs or takes (honestly) → sign-off that smiles and points at a call.

## Tells that it's AI-written (stop if you see these)

- Three-item lists where everything starts with an "-ing" verb ("Driving growth, building trust, delivering results…")
- "Whether you're a startup, an agency, or an enterprise…"
- Paragraphs that end on a rhetorical flourish instead of a fact
- The words "comprehensive", "robust", "holistic", "landscape", "journey", "elevate", "delve"
- Bullets that are all full sentences ending in a period
- A closing line that restates the opening
- Every paragraph the same length
- **Zero jokes** — see `humour.md`

If you catch any of these, delete the paragraph and start again.

## One-line summary

**Write like Matt explaining over coffee why your site isn't booking calls — kind to you, a little outraged on your behalf, and physically unable to resist a metaphor.**

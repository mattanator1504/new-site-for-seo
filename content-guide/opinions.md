# Opinions & Hot Takes

> Matthew has strong, specific opinions about websites, SEO and outbound. Use them where relevant.
> These are what separate a real operator's voice from generic agency content.
> Every opinion here is drawn from how Matthew already positions his work. Add your own over time — but only ones you'd say out loud on a sales call.

## On agencies and the industry

- **You should talk to the person doing the work.** Not an account manager. Not a ticket queue. Matthew runs every project himself and stays the direct contact from first call to launch. If you can't get the person building your thing on a call, that's information.
- **Deliverables are not results.** "We launched your site" is a deliverable. "Your site booked 14 calls last month" is a result. Matthew ties every project to leads and pipeline. Anyone reporting impressions when you asked about booked calls is changing the subject.
- **You shouldn't have to buy everything.** Each service stands on its own. Hire one. The bundle (Full Growth System) is what he recommends for the strongest results, but he'll happily tell you to start with one piece.
- **One person coordinating beats four vendors pointing at each other.** When the web person blames the SEO person who blames the email person, the lead is what falls through the gap.

## On websites

- **"Looks good" is not the job.** A site that looks fine but doesn't generate inquiries is an expensive brochure. The job is getting the right people to book a call or submit a form.
- **Traffic without inquiries is a messaging and flow problem, not a traffic problem.** Don't buy more visitors for a site that isn't converting the ones it has.
- **Every page needs one obvious next step.** If a visitor has to hunt for how to talk to you, most won't.
- **Measure visitor-to-lead conversion, or you're guessing.** Tracking goes in at launch, not "later."

## On SEO

- **SEO compounds. It is not fast.** Anyone promising page one in 30 days is either lying or targeting a keyword nobody searches. Matthew sets realistic expectations upfront.
- **Rank for what buyers actually search.** High-buying-intent keywords beat high-volume vanity keywords. Fifty searches a month from people ready to buy beats 5,000 from people doing homework.
- **Fix the foundations before writing content.** Technical issues, structure and speed first. Content on a broken site is a nice painting in a house with no floor.
- **For local service businesses, the Google Business Profile and map pack are half the game.** Often the faster half.

## On outbound

- **Never send cold email from your main domain.** Separate sending domains, SPF/DKIM set up properly, warmed safely before launch. Burn a secondary domain and it's an inconvenience. Burn your main one and your invoices stop landing.
- **Outbound needs a proven offer.** Cold email amplifies what you've got. If the offer doesn't close warm leads, it won't close cold ones.
- **Sending is the easy part. Follow-up is where deals die.** Every reply needs a fast response and a path to a booked call — SMS and email follow-up, calendar booking, confirmations, reminders.
- **Outbound takes weeks to ramp.** Domains need warming. Anyone promising meetings in week one is skipping steps that protect you.
- **Relying only on referrals or paid ads is fragile.** Referrals dry up without warning. Ads stop the day the card does. Outbound is a channel you control.

## On speed-to-lead

- **Reply in minutes, not days.** Contacting a lead within 5 minutes increases conversion dramatically. Matthew builds CRM setups with sub-2-minute response times so no lead sits waiting.
- **A dropped lead costs more than a bad lead.** You already paid to get them. Losing them to a slow reply is paying twice for nothing.

## On selling (how Matthew sells)

- **No pitch, no pressure.** The first call is 15 minutes. He looks at your setup and tells you what he'd fix first, whether or not you work together.
- **Price before work.** You get a clear recommendation and what it costs before anything starts.
- **Tell people when NOT to hire you.** If someone's offer isn't proven yet, outbound will waste their money. Say so. This is the single biggest voice tell. Generic agency content never does it.

## How to use opinions in content

- **One strong opinion per post, max.** Deploy it where it makes the post feel human.
- **Back every opinion with a number or a story.** "Speed matters" is a bumper sticker. "Within 5 minutes increases conversion dramatically, which is why I build for under 2" is an opinion.
- **Opinions are about behaviours, never named competitors.**
